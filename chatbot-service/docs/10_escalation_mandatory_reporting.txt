SECTION 1: EMERGENCY ESCALATION THRESHOLDS
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The following conditions trigger IMMEDIATE escalation regardless of case risk score:

E1 — Immediate Physical Danger:
- Youth has expressed intent to harm self or others within the next 24 hours
- Youth has taken a harmful action (ingested substances, inflicted injury requiring medical attention)
- Youth is in an unsafe physical location (missing, homeless, with a dangerous individual)
ACTION: Call 995 immediately. Notify SCS supervisor within 30 minutes. Complete Critical Incident Report (CIR) within 24 hours.

E2 — Active Suicidal Crisis:
- Youth has a specific plan, means, and timeline for suicide
- Youth is uncontactable after making suicidal statements
- A peer has contacted SCS reporting imminent risk
ACTION: Follow Suicidal Ideation Protocol (03). Call police welfare check. Parent notification within 15 minutes if possible.

E3 — Active Child Abuse:
- Youth reports current physical, sexual, or severe emotional abuse in the home
- Physical evidence of abuse is visible (bruises, burns, injuries)
- Youth is being kept home against their will by an abusive guardian
ACTION: Do not wait. Call MSF CPS at 1800-111-2222. Document evidence immediately. Do not confront abuser.

E4 — Sexual Exploitation:
- Evidence that youth is being sexually exploited online or offline
- Youth has shared sexual content with an adult under coercion
- Suspected trafficking situation
ACTION: Report to SPF immediately. Contact Internet Crimes Against Children (ICAC) for online cases. Preserve all evidence without forwarding. Involve SCS supervisor immediately.

E5 — Serious Mental Health Decompensation:
- Youth appears to be experiencing psychosis (hallucinations, delusions, disorganised behaviour)
- Youth has severe dissociation or is unable to care for themselves
ACTION: Refer to IMH A&E at Buangkok immediately. Parent notification. Accompany youth if possible and safe.

SECTION 2: MANDATORY REPORTING FRAMEWORK
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Under Singapore law, specific categories of professionals are mandatory reporters for child abuse. SCS social workers and helpers fall under this obligation.

2.1 Child Abuse Reporting (CYPA)
Report to: MSF Child Protective Service — 1800-111-2222
When: Reasonable suspicion of abuse, neglect, or exploitation (certainty is NOT required)
Timeline: Immediately upon identifying concern

2.2 Sexual Offences Against Minors (Penal Code)
Report to: Singapore Police Force — 999
When: Any disclosure or evidence of sexual offence against a person under 16 years old
Timeline: Same day

2.3 Protection from Harassment Act (POHA)
Report to: Singapore Police Force — 999 or Magistrates' Courts
When: Harassment involves criminal threats, stalking, or coordinated harassment
Timeline: Within 48 hours for non-imminent situations

2.4 Trafficking in Persons
Report to: SPF or National Crime Prevention Council human trafficking hotline — 1800-325-1234
When: Evidence of trafficking indicators

2.5 Platform Reporting
All cases with evidence of illegal content (CSAM, grooming) on social media must also report directly to platform Trust and Safety teams and to the Internet Watch Foundation (for CSAM).

Documentation Requirement:
All mandatory reports must be logged in the SCS case management system with:
- Date and time of report
- Report reference number (if provided)
- Name of officer/caseworker at receiving agency
- Copy of any written report submitted

SECTION 3: CASE RISK SCORE REVIEW TRIGGERS
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Risk scores are automatically reviewed by the SCS monitoring system. Manual review is required when:
- Risk score increases by 2+ points in a single monitoring cycle
- New signal type not previously detected appears
- Youth's case status changes (new school, new living situation, new relationship)
- Helper closes a contact and documents significant change

Risk score changes must be documented with:
- Previous score
- New score
- Specific signals driving the change
- Actions taken in response

SECTION 4: CASE ASSIGNMENT GUIDELINES
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

4.1 Workload Limits
Standard caseload: maximum 10 active cases per helper.
High-complexity caseload (risk 4–5 cases): maximum 6 active cases.
After caseload limit reached: reassignment of new cases to other helpers.

4.2 Assignment Criteria
Consider:
- Language and cultural match (e.g., Malay-speaking helper for Malay-speaking youth)
- Existing relationship (continuity of care where safe)
- Specialisation (some helpers certified for crisis work only)
- Gender preference (particularly for SOGI or sexual exploitation cases)
- Geographic proximity (for in-person visits)

4.3 Reassignment Protocol
A helper may request reassignment if:
- They have a pre-existing relationship with the youth or family (conflict of interest)
- The case requires specialist skills the helper does not have
- Caseload is at maximum capacity
- There is a personal safety concern for the helper

All reassignment requests must be approved by the SCS supervisor.

SECTION 5: CASE CLOSURE CRITERIA
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

A case may be closed when ALL of the following conditions are met:
1. No new risk signals in the past 60 days (30 days for low-risk cases)
2. Youth reports satisfactory wellbeing on final assessment
3. Protective factors are in place (support network, clinical care if needed)
4. School or community reports stability
5. Parent/guardian satisfied or engaged with ongoing community support
6. No pending mandatory reports or follow-up actions
7. Helper has written a case closure summary and obtained supervisor sign-off

Note: Closure does not mean monitoring ends entirely. Youth may be placed in "passive monitoring" mode for 90 days post-closure.

Cases may NOT be closed when:
- Youth is still engaged with a clinical service for the presenting issue
- A mandatory report has been filed and outcome is unknown
- Youth is in a shelter or protective custody
- Risk score was 4 or 5 within the last 30 days

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION 6: RECURRENCE MANAGEMENT
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Definition: Recurrence = the same risk category flagging in a new monitoring cycle after a previous case for the same youth was closed or de-escalated.

Protocol for recurrence:
- 1st recurrence: Re-open case. Review what changed. Update intervention plan.
- 2nd recurrence: Case conference with supervisor. Review whether current approach is working.
- 3rd recurrence: Mandatory clinical review. Consider intensive case management.
- 4th+ recurrence: Multi-agency case conference. Consider residential programme or intensive community intervention.
