## SCS OUTREACH MESSAGE TEMPLATES

These are starting-point templates. Always personalise before sending. Never send verbatim automated messages. You know the context; the AI does not reach out — you do.

=== TEMPLATE CATEGORY: INITIAL CONTACT ===

TEMPLATE A — General Warm Check-In:
Subject: (none — DM format)
"Hi [First Name], I'm [Your Name] from YOUTHHCARE at the Singapore Children's Society. I wanted to reach out and just check in with you. You don't have to share anything you're not ready to — I'm just here to listen if you ever need. Hope you're doing okay."

When to use: First contact for any case. Warm, low-pressure, no assumption of problem.
Personalisation tips: Use their preferred name if known. Avoid clinical language.

TEMPLATE B — After a Difficult Event:
"Hi [First Name], I heard things have been a bit tough lately. Please know that there are people who genuinely care, and support is always here whenever you're ready for it. No pressure at all."

When to use: After a specific incident has been detected (post-conflict, harassment incident).
Personalisation tips: Reference the general situation vaguely if appropriate. Do not reference specific posts or AI detection.

TEMPLATE C — Follow-Up (No Response Received):
"Just wanted to let you know I'm still here. There's no rush — take whatever time you need. I'm around if you ever want to talk."

When to use: 24–48h after initial contact with no response. Do not send more than twice.
Personalisation tips: Keep it shorter on second attempt. Do not pressure.

TEMPLATE D — Follow-Up (Some Engagement):
"Thanks for chatting with me earlier. I just wanted to check in and see how you're going since we last spoke. How have things been?"

When to use: After a conversation has been established. Maintain continuity.
Personalisation tips: Reference something specific from the previous conversation to show you listened.

=== TEMPLATE CATEGORY: CATEGORY-SPECIFIC ===

TEMPLATE E — Self-Harm / Distress:
"Hi [First Name], I won't pretend I'm not a little worried about how you're doing. I just wanted to say — you don't have to go through whatever you're feeling alone. There are people who care about you and want to help. Would you be open to having a chat sometime?"

When to use: Risk ≥ 4, distress language flagged. Use with care. Review trauma-informed guidelines first.
Do NOT use: If the youth has explicitly mentioned active self-harm planning — escalate first, then outreach.
Personalisation tips: Be direct about concern, but not alarmist. Avoid the word "crisis."

TEMPLATE F — Cyberbullying:
"Hi [First Name], I know online spaces can get really rough sometimes, and what you've been going through doesn't sound easy. I want you to know that what's happening is not okay, and you deserve support. If you'd like to talk to someone about what's going on, I'm here."

When to use: Cyberbullying cases, after initial review.
Personalisation tips: Do not reveal how you detected the issue.

TEMPLATE G — Bullying (School-Based):
"Hi [First Name], I heard things have been difficult with some peers. Just know that you don't deserve to be treated badly by anyone — full stop. If you want to talk to someone who gets it, I'm here for you."

When to use: Bullying cases, particularly peer exclusion or verbal bullying.
Personalisation tips: Keep language age-appropriate. Validate without taking sides.

TEMPLATE H — Family Conflict:
"Hi [First Name], home situations can be really complicated and stressful, and I imagine things haven't been the easiest recently. You deserve to feel safe and heard. I'm here if you want to talk — no judgment, no pressure."

When to use: Family conflict cases. Do not reference specific AI signals.
Important note: If safety is a concern, escalate before outreach.
Personalisation tips: Keep it brief. The goal is to open a door, not provide advice in the first message.

TEMPLATE I — Loneliness / Social Isolation:
"Hi [First Name], just thought I'd drop you a message to say hello and check in. Sometimes things get quiet and it's easy to feel like you're on your own. Just so you know, there are people here who are thinking of you."

When to use: Isolation and loneliness cases.
Personalisation tips: Warm and low-pressure. Never imply the youth is "isolated" — they may not have self-identified this way.

TEMPLATE J — Academic Stress:
"Hi [First Name], exam season is tough for so many people, and it sounds like things have been really full-on for you lately. Just wanted to check in and see how you're doing — and let you know there's support available if you ever need it."

When to use: Academic stress cases.
Personalisation tips: Acknowledge the stress without making them feel like it's being monitored.

=== TEMPLATE CATEGORY: RESOURCE SHARING ===

TEMPLATE K — Sharing Crisis Line:
"I just wanted to share this in case it's ever useful — you can reach the Samaritans of Singapore at 1800-221-4444 (free, 24/7). Sometimes it helps to just talk to someone. No problem is too small."

Use only: After an initial relationship has been established. Never as a first message.

TEMPLATE L — Sharing General Support:
"There are some really good online support spaces for young people — CHAT (https://www.chat.mentalhealth.sg) has free and confidential services. Might be worth knowing about, even just for a rainy day."

=== TEMPLATE CATEGORY: ESCALATION COMMUNICATION ===

TEMPLATE M — Informing Youth of Escalation (when appropriate + consented):
"Hi [First Name], I just wanted to be upfront with you — because I care about you, I'm going to let my team lead know about our conversation so we can make sure you have the best support possible. Nothing you've said will be shared beyond our team without talking to you first."

Use only: When escalation has been decided and youth has some level of engagement.
Do not use: If escalating without youth knowledge (contact team lead first in that case).

=== ADDITIONAL GUIDELINE: PLATFORM-SPECIFIC NOTES ===

Instagram DM:
- Keep first messages short (2–3 sentences max). Long messages can feel overwhelming.
- Do not use multiple bullet points or large walls of text in the first contact.
- Use a warm, conversational tone — not formal or clinical.
- Check account privacy settings before sending: if the account is set to receive messages from non-followers, use the existing request format.

Second Attempt:
- If no response after first message, wait at least 24 hours before second attempt.
- Do not send more than 2 unsolicited messages before escalating or closing initial contact attempt.

Response Handling:
- If a youth responds positively: log in checklist, move to active engagement, schedule follow-up.
- If a youth responds negatively (e.g., "leave me alone"): respect their space, log in checklist, monitor score — do not reply unless risk escalates.
- If a youth responds in distress: shift to immediate support mode, escalate if needed.
