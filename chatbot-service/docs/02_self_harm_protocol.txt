SCHOOL OF CHILD SAFEGUARDING (SCS) — PROTOCOL DOCUMENT
Title: Self-Harm Detection, Assessment and Intervention Protocol
Category: Self-Harm Risk
Version: 4.2 | Last Updated: February 2025
Classification: CONFIDENTIAL — Trained Social Workers Only

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION 1: PURPOSE AND SCOPE
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

This protocol governs the response to all cases where youth are identified as engaging in non-suicidal self-injury (NSSI) or where self-harm risk is flagged through social media monitoring, peer referral, school referral, or direct disclosure.

Self-harm (also called self-injury or NSSI) refers to intentional, direct injury of a person's body without suicidal intent. It includes cutting, burning, scratching, hitting, or other forms of physical self-injury. It is distinct from suicidal ideation but may escalate to it. Handle all cases with sensitivity and without judgment.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION 2: INITIAL DETECTION AND TRIAGE
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

2.1 Social Media Signals (flagged by SCS monitoring system)
- Visible wounds or scars in photos (wrists, arms, thighs)
- Cryptic captions expressing emotional pain, numbness, relief through pain
- Posts referencing self-harm methods or materials
- Hashtags or community engagement in pro-self-harm content
- Late-night posting spikes with distress themes

2.2 Direct Disclosure
- Youth may disclose during outreach calls or messages to helpers.
- Do not express shock, alarm, or judgment.
- Acknowledge the disclosure: "Thank you for telling me. I care about your safety."
- Move immediately to safety assessment.

2.3 Peer Referral
- Take all peer referrals seriously.
- Do not dismiss as attention-seeking.
- Peer may also require support — assess their wellbeing separately.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION 3: RISK ASSESSMENT — COLUMBIA PROTOCOL LITE
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Use the following questions to assess risk level during the first contact:

Q1: "Have you hurt yourself on purpose recently?"
Q2: "How often does this happen?"
Q3: "How serious were the injuries? Did you need medical attention?"
Q4: "Are you having any thoughts of suicide or wanting to die?"
Q5: "Is there anything at home or school making this worse?"

Scoring:
- Risk Level 1 (Low): Infrequent, superficial, no suicidal ideation, stable home environment.
- Risk Level 2 (Medium): Recurrent, moderate severity, some suicidal thoughts without plan.
- Risk Level 3 (High): Frequent, escalating severity, active suicidal thoughts.
- Risk Level 4 (Critical): Serious physical harm, active suicide plan or intent.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION 4: IMMEDIATE RESPONSE PROCEDURES
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

4.1 Risk Level 1 — Low
- Provide empathetic listening and psychoeducation.
- Create a safety plan together (identify triggers, coping alternatives, trusted adults).
- Schedule follow-up within 7 days.
- Refer to school counsellor.

4.2 Risk Level 2 — Medium
- All above plus parent/guardian notification within 24 hours (with youth's consent where possible).
- Refer to community mental health service for assessment.
- Weekly follow-up for minimum 4 weeks.
- School to be informed at helper's discretion based on consent.

4.3 Risk Level 3 — High
- Immediate family notification.
- Arrange clinical psychology assessment within 48 hours.
- Hospital emergency department referral if wounds require treatment.
- Daily follow-up for first 7 days.
- Case to be reviewed by SCS supervisor.

4.4 Risk Level 4 — Critical
- Treat as per Suicidal Ideation Protocol (see Protocol 02).
- Emergency services (995) if immediate physical danger.
- Document all actions and escalate to SCS clinical team immediately.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION 5: CREATING A SAFETY PLAN
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The safety plan should be co-created with the youth and include:
1. Warning signs (feelings, thoughts, situations that precede self-harm)
2. Internal coping strategies (what the youth can do alone: walk, music, draw)
3. Social contacts who can distract (friends, not crisis-specific)
4. Trusted adults to contact (name and number)
5. Crisis helplines (Samaritans of Singapore: 1-767; IMH: 6389 2222)
6. Means restriction: safely store or remove sharp objects where possible
7. Agreed actions if plan fails

Print or share the plan digitally. Revisit and update at each contact.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION 6: WORKING WITH PARENTS AND GUARDIANS
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Parents often react with shock, guilt, or anger. Prepare them with:
- Psychoeducation: self-harm is a coping mechanism, not attention-seeking.
- Guidance on communication: avoid confrontation, express concern calmly.
- Instructions: do not punish. Do not remove all access to the youth's room or devices immediately.
- Resources: Parent support groups, Family Service Centres (FSCs).

Obtain consent from youth before informing parents where the youth is 14 years or older and there is no immediate safety risk. If youth is below 14, parental notification is mandatory.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION 7: RECURRING CASES
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Recurring self-harm (same youth, multiple cycles within 12 months) requires:
- Case review with SCS supervisor after 2nd recurrence.
- Mandatory clinical referral after 3rd recurrence.
- Consideration of intensive outpatient programme.
- Review of current helper relationship — consider reassignment if stuck.
- Document escalation trajectory in case history with all cycle timestamps.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION 8: HELPER SELF-CARE
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Working with self-harm cases is emotionally taxing. Helpers should:
- Debrief with supervisor after each high-risk contact.
- Use SCS Employee Assistance Programme (EAP) when needed.
- Not handle more than 3 self-harm cases simultaneously without supervisor consultation.
- Declare secondary traumatic stress symptoms to supervisor immediately.
