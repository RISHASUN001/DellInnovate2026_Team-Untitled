SCHOOL OF CHILD SAFEGUARDING (SCS) — PROTOCOL DOCUMENT
Title: Online Grooming and Child Sexual Exploitation Prevention Protocol
Category: Grooming Risk / Child Sexual Exploitation
Version: 3.0 | Last Updated: April 2025
Classification: RESTRICTED — Supervisors and Senior Helpers Only

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION 1: DEFINITIONS
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Online grooming is the process by which an individual (usually an adult) builds a relationship of trust and emotional connection with a child or young person online, with the intent of sexually exploiting them. This includes:
- Sexual grooming: building rapport to later solicit sexual content or meetings
- Financial grooming: gifts, gaming credits, cash in exchange for compliance or secrecy
- Trafficking grooming: recruiting youth into exploitative situations

Key platforms of concern in Singapore: Instagram, TikTok, Discord, online gaming platforms (Roblox, Minecraft), dating apps accessed by underage users.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION 2: DETECTION INDICATORS
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Social media and behavioural signals that indicate grooming risk:

Digital signals (flagged by SCS monitoring system):
- Interaction with accounts flagged for predatory behaviour
- Comments or DMs from adults significantly older than the youth
- Youth posting about receiving gifts from adults they met online
- Secrecy references ("he said not to tell anyone", "our secret")
- Youth posting about a romantic relationship with an adult
- Rapid withdrawal from family / known peer groups

Behavioural signals (reported by school or family):
- Unexplained gifts, money, or gaming credits
- Secretive phone/device use, particularly late at night
- Mood changes after device use
- New adult "friends" that family has never met
- Reluctance to discuss online activities

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION 3: IMMEDIATE RESPONSE — RISK LEVELS
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Risk Level 1 (Monitoring): Suspicious interactions but no confirmed grooming. No gifts, no secrecy.
→ Increase monitoring frequency. Gentle psychoeducation with youth about online safety.

Risk Level 2 (Moderate Concern): Confirmed adult-youth online friendship with age gap >5 years. Some gift-giving.
→ Parent notification within 24 hours. Youth interview to assess relationship extent. Online safety session.

Risk Level 3 (High Risk): Confirmed grooming pattern. Sexual content may have been solicited. Youth at risk.
→ Mandatory SPF report. Parent notification. Youth removed from contact with perpetrator (accounts blocked). Clinical referral.

Risk Level 4 (Critical — Child Sexual Exploitation): Confirmed sexual content exchange, meeting, or trafficking indicators.
→ Immediate SPF report. Do not investigate further — preserve evidence and hand to SPF. Clinical trauma support activated. Media/device evidence secured.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION 4: MANDATORY POLICE REPORTING
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Under the Children and Young Persons Act (CYPA) and Penal Code Section 376E (Sexual Grooming of Minors), all cases at Risk Level 3 and above must be reported to SPF.

To file a report:
- Call SPF at 999 or visit the nearest police station.
- For online sexual exploitation: contact the Internet Crimes Against Children (ICAC) unit.
- Preserve ALL digital evidence (screenshots, DM records, platform URLs).
- Do not confront the suspected perpetrator — this may alert them and destroy evidence.

Evidence chain of custody:
- Do not forward screenshots to personal devices.
- Upload evidence directly to the SCS case management system.
- Label all evidence with date, time, platform, and account ID.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION 5: WORKING WITH THE YOUTH
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Youth who are groomed often do not see themselves as victims. They may:
- Have genuine emotional attachment to the perpetrator.
- Fear getting the perpetrator "in trouble."
- Feel shame, embarrassment, or guilt.
- Be resistant to intervention.

Helper approach:
1. Avoid language like "victim," "predator," or "abuse" initially — this may cause defensiveness.
2. Build trust. Do not rush the disclosure process.
3. Use open-ended questions: "Tell me about this person you've been talking to."
4. Normalise the situation: "This happens to a lot of young people."
5. Introduce online safety concepts neutrally: "Sometimes adults can be manipulative online."
6. If youth discloses: thank them, validate their bravery, reassure there is no blame.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION 6: PSYCHOEDUCATION — ONLINE SAFETY
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Provide age-appropriate psychoeducation covering:
- Safe vs. unsafe adults online
- Warning signs of grooming (too much attention, secrecy, gifts)
- Digital safety: privacy settings, blocking, screenshot-reporting
- Encouraging youth to talk to trusted adults if uncomfortable
- Resources: Media Literacy Council (Singapore), Touch Cyber Wellness

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION 7: POST-INTERVENTION SUPPORT
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Youth who have been groomed or exploited may experience:
- Trauma responses (dissociation, avoidance, intrusive memories)
- Shame and self-blame
- Complex attachment feelings toward perpetrator

Refer to:
- IMH Child Guidance Clinic (CGC) for trauma-informed therapy
- AWARE Sexual Assault Care Centre (SACC): 1800-774-5935
- TOUCH Youth Services for peer support groups
