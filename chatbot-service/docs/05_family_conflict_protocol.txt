SCHOOL OF CHILD SAFEGUARDING (SCS) — PROTOCOL DOCUMENT
Title: Family Conflict and Domestic Distress Intervention Protocol
Category: Family Conflict
Version: 2.8 | Last Updated: January 2025

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION 1: SCOPE
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

This protocol covers cases where youth are experiencing significant distress related to family conflict, including parental discord, domestic violence, parent-child conflict, sibling aggression, and neglect indicators.

Family conflict in youth is associated with: increased self-harm risk, academic disengagement, running away, substance use, and poor mental health outcomes. Early intervention is critical.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION 2: RISK STRATIFICATION
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Level 1 — Low: Occasional parental argument. Youth venting. No physical conflict. Youth feels safe.
→ Monitor. Provide general family communication resources.

Level 2 — Moderate: Regular conflict affecting youth's wellbeing. Parent-child communication breakdown. Youth staying away from home.
→ Refer to Family Service Centre (FSC) within 1 week. Assess youth's safety plan at home.

Level 3 — High: Physical altercations involving youth (either witnessing or participating). Threats of harm. Evidence of emotional abuse. Youth expressing desire to run away.
→ Assess for immediate safety. If youth at imminent risk at home, consider referral to youth shelter. Contact MSF Child Protective Service (CPS) if abuse suspected.

Level 4 — Critical: Confirmed physical abuse, domestic violence involving youth, neglect (food/shelter insecurity), or abandonment.
→ Mandatory report to MSF CPS (1800-111-2222). Police report if physical harm occurred. Youth may be placed in protective custody.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION 3: ASSESSMENT APPROACH
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

3.1 Youth Interview
Conduct separately from family (privacy is essential). Key questions:
- "How would you describe the situation at home?"
- "Do you feel safe at home?"
- "Has anyone at home ever hurt you physically?"
- "Do you have enough food, clothing, and a safe place to sleep?"
- "Who do you go to when things are difficult at home?"

3.2 Family Interview (where safe and appropriate)
- Conduct with parent or guardian.
- Be neutral, non-accusatory.
- Explore stressors: financial, employment, housing, health.
- Provide resources for parental support.

3.3 Digital Signal Assessment
Social media posts indicating family conflict risk:
- References to physical conflict at home
- Expressing desire to run away or never return home
- References to food insecurity or homelessness
- Staying out late or overnight to avoid home
- References to parental addiction, mental health, or domestic violence

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION 4: INTERVENTION PATHWAYS
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

4.1 Family Service Centres (FSCs)
- Refer family to nearest FSC for counselling and casework.
- FSCs offer family therapy, financial assistance guidance, and community programmes.
- List of FSCs: www.msf.gov.sg/FSCLocator

4.2 Youth Emergency Shelters
If youth cannot safely return home:
- Boys Town (boys): 6858 7900
- SHINE Children and Youth Services: 6709 4500
- Covenant Family Centre: 6388 4747
- Refer through MSF SafeConnect portal.

4.3 Mediation Services
- Community Mediation Centre (CMC): suitable for non-violent family disputes.
- Refer parent and youth jointly if relationship is recoverable.

4.4 Parent Support
- Family Life Champions Programme (MCCY)
- Financial counselling through ComCare

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION 5: CULTURAL CONSIDERATIONS
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Singapore's diverse population means family conflict may be shaped by cultural norms around:
- Parental authority and filial piety (especially in Chinese, Malay, Indian contexts)
- Corporal punishment as discipline (legally permissible in limited forms under Singapore law)
- Shame around seeking help outside the family

Helpers should:
- Not impose Western family norms.
- Acknowledge cultural context while assessing safety.
- Use a strength-based approach: identify protective cultural factors.
- Engage bilingual helpers where needed (Mandarin, Malay, Tamil).

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION 6: MANDATORY REPORTING — CHILD ABUSE
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Under the CYPA, all registered social workers and professionals have a duty to report suspected child abuse to the Director of Social Welfare (MSF). This includes:
- Physical abuse
- Sexual abuse
- Emotional/psychological abuse
- Neglect (inadequate food, shelter, supervision, medical care)

To make a report: Call MSF CPS at 1800-111-2222 (24/7). Provide:
- Youth's full name, age, school, address (if known)
- Nature of concerns
- Your name and contact number

You do not need certainty to report — a reasonable suspicion is sufficient.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION 7: RUNNING AWAY — PROTOCOL
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

If youth has run away or expressed intent to:
1. Contact parents to confirm youth's whereabouts.
2. If missing: file a police report immediately (999 or nearest station).
3. Contact youth's known peer network.
4. Check known gathering spots (social media may provide clues).
5. If youth makes contact with SCS, prioritise safety and offer shelter.

Do not pressure youth to return home if the home is assessed as unsafe.
