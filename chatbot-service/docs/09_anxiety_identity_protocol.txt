PART A: ANXIETY AND PANIC DISORDER PROTOCOL
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

SECTION A1: CLINICAL OVERVIEW

Anxiety is the most common mental health challenge in youth. It exists on a spectrum from normal adaptive anxiety to clinical anxiety disorders requiring treatment. SCS encounters anxiety cases where symptoms are severe enough to impair the youth's daily functioning and are visible through digital monitoring.

Common presentations:
- Generalised Anxiety Disorder (GAD): persistent worry across multiple life domains
- Social Anxiety Disorder: intense fear of social situations, peer judgment
- Panic Disorder: recurrent, unexpected panic attacks
- School-related anxiety (overlaps with academic stress protocol)

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION A2: DETECTION SIGNALS
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Social media indicators for anxiety:
- Describing physical panic symptoms (heart racing, can't breathe, dizzy)
- Posting about avoiding social situations or events
- Excessive reassurance-seeking behaviour in comments
- "Doom-scrolling" and catastrophic thinking posts
- Expressing fear about specific scenarios (germs, tests, embarrassment)
- Nighttime distress posts (anxiety often peaks at night)

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION A3: ASSESSMENT AND RESPONSE
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Severity Assessment using GAD-7 screening questions:
- Over the past 2 weeks, how often have you felt nervous, anxious, or on edge?
- How often have you been unable to stop or control worrying?
- How often has your anxiety prevented you from doing things you needed to do?

Score interpretation: 0–4 minimal; 5–9 mild; 10–14 moderate; 15–21 severe.

Moderate–Severe (score 10+): Refer to school counsellor + GP/REACH referral.

Immediate support during panic attack (by phone/chat):
1. "You are safe. This will pass. I'm here with you."
2. Box breathing: breathe in 4 counts, hold 4, out 4, hold 4.
3. 5-4-3-2-1 grounding: name 5 things you see, 4 you hear, 3 you can touch, 2 you smell, 1 taste.
4. Stay on the line until the acute episode passes (typically 5–20 minutes).
5. After: "You got through that. That took strength."

School Avoidance Due to Anxiety:
- Apply graduated exposure plan (see Academic Stress protocol Section 6).
- Coordinate with school counsellor to create a "safe space" at school.
- Provide accommodation letters for exam anxiety where clinically indicated.

PART B: IDENTITY CRISIS SUPPORT PROTOCOL
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

SECTION B1: OVERVIEW

Identity development is a normal part of adolescence. An identity crisis becomes a safeguarding concern when it:
- Is causing significant psychological distress
- Is linked to family rejection or conflict
- Is associated with peer victimisation (e.g., due to gender identity or religion)
- Has elevated self-harm or suicidal ideation risk

Identity domains relevant to SCS cases:
- Sexual orientation and gender identity (SOGI)
- Religious identity conflict
- Cultural/ethnic identity (particularly common in Singapore's multiracial context)
- Disability or neurodivergent identity

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION B2: LGBTQ+ YOUTH — SPECIFIC GUIDANCE
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Research consistently shows that LGBTQ+ youth who are rejected by their families have 8x higher risk of suicidal ideation. Family acceptance is the single strongest protective factor.

When working with LGBTQ+ youth:
- Use the youth's preferred name and pronouns.
- Do not attempt to change the youth's orientation or identity.
- Do not disclose the youth's identity to parents without the youth's explicit consent.
- Assess family environment before any family engagement.
- If disclosure to parents is requested: prepare the youth and explore likely family response.

Family support resources:
- Oogachaga Counselling and Support: 3138-0366 (LGBTQ+ specific, SG)
- PFLAG Singapore: support for families of LGBTQ+ individuals
- Counselling by an LGBTQ+-affirming therapist (refer through Oogachaga directory)

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION B3: RELIGIOUS IDENTITY CONFLICT
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Common in Singapore: youth navigating tension between personal beliefs and family/community religious expectations.

Approach:
- Do not make judgments about religious beliefs (helper's own or the youth's).
- Explore the specific tension: "What feels most uncomfortable for you about this?"
- Identify what the youth wants (to leave religion, to modify practice, to find integration).
- Connect with religious youth counsellors where appropriate (some mosques, churches offer secular-informed counselling).

Do not facilitate any actions that put youth at risk of family violence or abandonment.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION B4: CULTURAL AND ETHNIC IDENTITY
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Singapore's multiracial context means some youth navigate tension between heritage culture and peer culture. Issues include:
- Language: heritage language use and pressure
- Interracial relationships and family disapproval
- Code-switching stress
- Racial microaggressions from peers

Helper approach: validate the complexity, avoid simplistic advice, explore the youth's own vision for their identity.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION B5: RISK ELEVATION IN IDENTITY CASES
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Identity-related cases should be escalated when:
- Family rejection is explicit and severe (youth has been asked to leave home)
- Peer victimisation is occurring due to identity
- Youth expresses hopelessness about ever being accepted
- Self-harm indicators are present alongside identity distress

Apply the relevant protocol (Self-Harm, Suicidal Ideation, Family Conflict) in addition to identity support.
